# Binding Physics — What We Simplified

## The shell
8% frequency ratio + spatial proximity + polarity matching + decade
isolation. Deterministic: if two objects meet the criteria, they bind.

## What a non-thin version requires
- **Phase-locking dynamics**: real binding involves continuous phase
  coupling over time, not instantaneous check-and-bind. Two oscillators
  approach, couple, and may fail to lock. Our binding is a discrete
  event with no coupling history.
- **Energy conservation**: binding should release energy (lower
  frequency product) or require energy input. Our binding creates a
  node with freq = f1+f2, which is additive, not physically motivated.
- **Angular momentum**: vibrations in 3D carry orientation. Binding
  should depend on relative phase angle, not just frequency ratio.
- **Multi-body binding**: real chemistry has 3-body reactions. Our
  cascade is strictly pairwise (2 objects → 1 product).

## What breaks if wrong
If the simplification is wrong, the binding cascade produces structures
that would not form under realistic physics. The hierarchy would be an
artifact of the rule system, not an emergent property. Specifically:
atoms (level 4) would not correspond to anything physically stable.

## When to revisit
When the cascade reaches structures that must exhibit specific physical
properties (conductivity, elasticity, membrane permeability) that
depend on how binding actually works. Currently: not yet — we're still
verifying that the cascade produces stable hierarchies at all.
